# Design brief — extracted from the reference library

Working document. Step 3 prompts should quote from this rather than re-deriving it.

---

## Intent

An Engineering Physics student portfolio aimed at recruiters hiring **mechanical
engineering interns**.

- **Primary action:** read a project case study
- **Secondary action:** make contact
- Content is dense and technical: long-form writeups, CAD sections, shop photography,
  engineering drawings, short mechanism videos. One project alone carries 24 media items.

---

## The structural mismatch (read this first)

Eight of the nine references are **freelancer portfolios selling services to clients**.
Their job is to sell a person: a face at hero scale, a name in enormous type,
"Let's Talk", service lists, testimonials, client logos. Personality is the product
and content is thin.

This site has the opposite shape. The product is **the work**, the personality is
context, and the content is heavy.

Taken literally, these references would push James's face and name to full-screen and
bury the engineering below the fold — worse than what exists today. So:

> **Borrow the aesthetic. Do not borrow the structure.**
>
> Take the typography, the palettes, the spatial confidence, the photographic calm.
> Leave the sales architecture behind.

---

## Convergent signals — what the references agree on

1. **"Clean" is the recurring word.** Used unprompted for image2, image8, image9.
   Reads as: high contrast, uncluttered, obvious hierarchy, nothing decorative.
2. **Large confident headlines.** Four of nine lead with an oversized display line
   (image2, image4 "Projects.", image8, image9). Type is the primary graphic element,
   not an accessory to it.
3. **Photographic ground reads as premium.** image5 and image6 both use a large
   environmental photograph as the hero surface — described as "premium" and
   "calming". James already owns strong imagery: CAD renders, machine photography,
   shop floor.
4. **Light grounds dominate the favourites.** image2 (cream), image4 (off-white),
   image8 (white) are the three called "clean" without reservation.
5. **Generous negative space** in every reference praised without caveat.

## Divergent signals — where the references disagree

- **Light vs dark.** image2/4/8 are light; image1/3/5/9 are dark; image6 is muted
  daylight. Unresolved — deliberately left for the Step 3 spread to settle.
- **Minimal vs decorated.** image2/4/8 are austere. image1/3/7 use gradients, glow,
  and blobs — the exact vocabulary this overhaul exists to escape. James hedged on all
  three ("not the raw text", "not necessarily copy and paste worthy", "omit those
  pages"), which suggests he responds to their *energy*, not their ornament.

## Guardrails

Derived from James's own notes rather than invented. Every item traces to a reaction
he actually had.

**Never:**

- Greeting-style opening copy — "Hi!", "Hello, I'm…", "Hey there" *(image1)*
- Small text scattered around the layout as decoration *(image4)* — this indicts the
  current site directly, which uses 11px mono labels in nine places
- Services, testimonials, client logos, pricing — he sells nothing *(image7)*
- Layouts that depend on a cutout studio headshot; he doesn't have one *(image9)*
- Purple/blue gradient washes, glow, 3D blobs, glassmorphism *(image1, image3)*
- Inter, and near-black + cyan — the defaults the current site already fell into
- AI-generated imagery of any kind. His own CAD and shop photography is better and,
  on an engineering portfolio, more credible.

**Always:**

- Type large enough to be read comfortably, and used with intent
- The work visible early — a recruiter should reach real engineering fast
- Real photography and CAD carrying the colour
- One accent at most

## Candidate directions for the Step 3 spread

Five deliberately divergent takes, each traceable to references, together covering the
light/dark question so the spread resolves it.

1. **Editorial White** *(image2, image4, image8)* — off-white ground, very large sans
   headline, near-black text, wide margins. Imagery supplies all colour. Confident and
   design-literate.
2. **Atmospheric Dark** *(image5, image6, image9)* — dark ground with a large CAD render
   or machine photograph as hero surface, one warm accent. The "premium, calming" note.
3. **Technical Paper** *(his domain, not the references)* — engineering-drawing
   vocabulary: paper ground, precise thin rules, a real title block, monospace used
   sparingly and at readable size. Specific to him in a way no reference is.
4. **Steel & Daylight** *(image6)* — muted warm neutrals, industrial photography, the
   orange-red of the polyurethane liner pulled from his own photos as accent.
5. **Heavy Type Minimal** *(image8, image9)* — massive condensed display type as the
   primary graphic device, near-monochrome, images in strict rectangles.

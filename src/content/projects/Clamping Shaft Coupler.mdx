This is a clamping style shaft coupler that connects the **gearbox output shaft** and the
**propeller shaft** in the first 4WD vehicle of UBC BAJA. It's designed to carry the full torque the
gearbox delivers and hold the two shafts concentric. 

## Overview
### Design Objectives

- **Weight optimization** — For a BAJA vehicle with limited horsepower, weight optimization
  is very crucial. The coupler has been designed to the minimum permissible size through FEA
  simulations.
- **Maintain concentricity** — The clamping style coupler maintains concentricity of the two
  connected shafts through clamping forces exerted by a set of bolts.
- **Strength** — The coupler has been designed to withstand the maximum torque delivered to
  the propeller shaft from the gearbox. Designed with **AISI 4130** and validated through FEA.
- **Serviceability** — The coupler must be easy to install and uninstall in order to
  service any of the propeller shaft components.

### The Coupler

The coupler is a single body rather than a pair of halves. A **partial slit** runs its length
so the material flexes under bolt clamping force, and a **lateral cut** at mid-length
separates the two clamping zones, letting each end close onto a slightly different shaft
diameter. 

Four **M4 bolts** pull the joint closed. Clearance holes through the upper half,
tapped holes below, each seated in a machined pocket that keeps the head below the outer
diameter. A **3/32″ × 3/16″ keyway** runs the full length of the coupler.

### Assembly Layout

The coupler bridges the gearbox output and the propeller shaft. On the **gearbox end** it clamps
directly onto the gearbox output shaft, carried over from the gearbox project. On the
**driveshaft end** it clamps onto a **driveshaft insert, an adapter (Part of this project)** that carries the key
and ties the driveshaft into the coupler. The key in each bore carries the torque, while the
bolt clamping load holds the joint concentric.

## Analysis
### Simulation Setup

I ran the stress study on the **full assembly** rather than the coupler alone, so the contact
behaviour and bolt preload that actually set the stress state were part of the model.

- Simulates full throttle for max torque delivered, **~81 Nm**.
- A roller fixture at the end of the gearbox shaft, and a fixture near the weldment area of
  the shaft insert. 81 Nm of torque applied from the gearbox shaft.
- Definition of **all contact points and types** for every part in the assembly.
- **Bolt connectors** to simulate clamping loads, with bolt settings sourced directly from the
  McMaster-Carr M4 bolt.

### Results

**Coupler:** minimum factor of safety (FOS) of **~1.84**, occurring near the bolt pockets —
above the aim FOS of ~1.5.

**Shaft insert:** minimum factor of safety (FOS) of **~3.03**, occurring near the keyseat and
weldment area.

## Outcome

The final design met our packaging constraints while cutting cost by roughly **40%** against
off-the-shelf alternatives.

Two issues surfaced during vehicle integration: an initial misalignment between the propeller
shaft and the gearbox output shaft, caused by tolerance stack-up across the full vehicle
assembly, and a keyseat failure on the insert following a partial gearbox failure.

I resolved the misalignment by adjusting the propeller shaft carrier bearing height, and
addressed the keyseat failure by changing the insert material to **AISI 4340**, hardened to
**HRC 40**. 
Despite these challenges, this project successfully powered the 4WD system throughout the
**2026 Oregon BAJA SAE competition**.

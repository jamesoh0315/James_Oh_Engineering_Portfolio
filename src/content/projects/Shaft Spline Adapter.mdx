This is a welded shaft adapter that connects the **slip spline** and the **propeller shaft**
in the first 4WD vehicle of UBC BAJA. It carries the full torque the gearbox delivers while
keeping the spline's grease fitting reachable for service.

## Overview
### Design Objectives

- **Weight optimization** — For a BAJA vehicle with limited horsepower, weight optimization
  is very crucial. The adapter has been designed to the minimum permissible size through FEA
  simulations.
- **Grease fitting clearance** — Slip splines require lubrication to function properly
  without excessive wear and noise. This adapter design exposes the grease fitting on the
  spline for future service.
- **Strength** — The adapter has been designed to withstand the maximum torque delivered to
  the propeller shaft from the gearbox. Designed with **AISI 4340** and validated through FEA.
- **Manufacturability** — Manufactured in house, so the design had to stay within the
  machinery and tooling available in the shop.

### The Adapter

The adapter is a single turned part that steps from a **1.5″ spline bore** down to a
**0.87″ OD** that seats inside the propeller shaft. A taper between the two diameters
gives a gradual transition instead of a sharp shoulder, avoiding the stress concentration
that would come with one. The spline end is bored out flat so the spline rests square
against it.

Two chamfered holes take **rosette welds** for extra stability. The spline end is cut back at
**55°**, which exposes the spline's grease fitting while leaving a longer edge to weld along.

## Analysis
### Simulation Setup

- Simulates full throttle for max torque delivered, **~81 Nm**.
- A fixture at the spline end, where it will be welded together.
- Torque applied at a **split line**, where it will be welded to the propeller shaft.

	height={300}
	caption="Fixture at the spline weld face, with torque applied at the split line."

/>

### Results

Minimum factor of safety (FOS) of **~6.117**, occurring near the welding point. Well above
the aim FOS of ~3.5.

The torque calculation and the FEA simulation shows the adapter could be made thinner, but that thickness isn't
suitable for manufacturing the part would deform while being clamped in the lathe chuck
and the vice.

## Outcome

The part was released as a single machined component in **AISI 4340**, dimensioned in inches
to a general tolerance of ±0.01, and cut in house on a manual lathe.

I was able to fabricate the part in-house entirely, involving **10+ hours of lathing / milling** operations. 

Without any issues identified during the testing stage, this project successfully powered the 4WD system throughout the
**2026 Oregon BAJA SAE competition**.

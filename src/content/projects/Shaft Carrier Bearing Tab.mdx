This is a weldment tab for the bearing that carries the **center propeller shaft** in the
first 4WD vehicle of UBC BAJA. It has to hold the bearing accurately enough to keep the
propeller shaft aligned, which puts as much weight on how it is welded as on how it is
shaped.

## Overview
### Design Objectives

- **Weight optimization** — For a BAJA vehicle with limited horsepower, weight optimization
  is very crucial. The tab has been designed to the minimum permissible size through FEA
  simulations.
- **Positional accuracy** — The positioning of the bearing is crucial to maintain the
  propeller shaft alignment. This tab uses multiple aligning features for assembly, and is
  welded with a custom jig for accurate placement.
- **Strength** — The tab has been designed to withstand the maximum stress in possible load
  cases. Designed with **AISI 4130 0.063″ sheet metal** and validated through FEA.
- **Manufacturability** — Manufactured in house, so the tab could not use complicated
  compound bends or any difficult sheet metal assembly.

### The Tab

Two mounting pads carry **11 mm bolt holes** — the standard "normal fit" clearance for the
M10 bolts the **SKF P2BL 100-TF** bearing requires — joined by a middle bridge that keeps
the tab from twisting and holds both bolt holes horizontally aligned.

Positional accuracy comes from two alignment features working on different axes. A
rectangular tongue sits square to the width of the bearing feet, and the edge flanges sit
square to their sides, so the bearing locates itself in both directions rather than being
measured into place.

## Analysis

Every case carries the same load: a supported net weight of **60 N** at the vehicle's maximum
acceleration of **3 G**, so roughly **180 N** at the tab. What changes between them is the
direction of that load and which surface it lands on.

### Load Case 1 — Normal

The primary case under normal conditions, where the weight of the shaft, bearing, and CV
pulls down on the tab under gravity. The load is applied to the split surface where the tab
contacts the bearing feet.

### Results

Minimum factor of safety (FOS) of **~2.077**, occurring in load case 3, the side load. Above
the aim FOS of ~1.5.

That comes from a peak von Mises stress of **221 MPa** against the material's **460 MPa**
yield, with the maximum landing at the base of the flange where the tab meets the frame.

## Outcome

Unfortunately, I never got a picture of the tab welded onto the car. By the time I thought
to take one, the surrounding components had made the carrier bearing tab almost impossible
to reach.

The harder half of the problem was placement, so the tab was welded off a **custom jig**
rather than measured in place. The jig fixed the tab relative to the jig table through an
array of bolt holes, and its own pieces used **tab-and-slot features** so they could be
waterjet cut separately, then assembled and welded together.

The jig's layout shifted slightly from this design as the bearing's position moved partway
through, which is the main thing I would change about the order of the work: the mounting
position needs to be settled before the fixture that depends on it gets built.

After those changes, the tab successfully supported the **P2BL 100-TF** bearing that carries
the propeller shaft, powering the 4WD system throughout the
**2026 Oregon BAJA SAE competition**.

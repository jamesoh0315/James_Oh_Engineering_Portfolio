## Outcome

Being an experimental project, not everything went according to plan. The spinner head and
the electrical system both worked as intended. The converter ran the motor and the heaters
together off one outlet, and the gear train held the head at its target speed. What fell
short was heat. The PTC elements could not melt sugar quickly enough to sustain strands, and
the machine as a whole ended up too unreliable to run start to finish.

To find out whether heat was the *only* problem, I pulled the PTC elements out and warmed
the spinner head externally with a heat gun instead. With a high enough temperature at the
head, the strands formed exactly as they were supposed to.

### What I would take away from it

**A temperature rating is not a heat budget.** I chose the PTC elements on their rated
output of 200 °C ± 10 °C and treated that number as sufficient. What actually decides
whether sugar melts is how much *power* the elements can push into it against everything
carrying heat away. 

**Isolating a subsystem beats repeating the whole test.** Swapping in the heat gun took an
afternoon and answered a question that several full runs had not. It is worth reaching for
that kind of substitution earlier, before assuming the fault is spread across the design.

**Building from parts on hand sets a ceiling.** The motor, heating element, frame, gears, and brackets were
repurposed from what I already had, which got the machine running quickly but left the
assembly less rigid and less repeatable than desired.

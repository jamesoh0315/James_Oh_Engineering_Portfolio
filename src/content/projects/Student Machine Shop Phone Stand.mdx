This is a project to practice hands-on machining, as part of the **Student Machine Shop
(SMS)** training course offered by the Department of Physics & Astronomy at UBC.

## Project Objectives

- **Machine safety** — The course covers basic safety rules around high-speed rotating
  machinery: working with a buddy, safety goggles, rolling sleeves up to avoid getting
  caught, properly securing stock, and so on.
- **Technical drawings and GD&T** — Reading and understanding technical drawings well enough
  to machine the parts accurately, and meeting the geometric tolerances the assembly
  requires.
- **Machine fundamentals** — A range of manufacturing techniques across the manual lathe,
  mill, waterjet cutter, bandsaw, hacksaw, and drill press, covering facing, turning,
  drilling, threading, auto feed, and edge finding.

## Completed Assembly

- All components were machined to the appropriate tolerance for a good fit.
- I learned the capabilities and limitations of each machine, which lets me start designing
  for manufacturing rather than designing in the abstract.
- I learned the different properties of the materials, especially strength, through working
  them directly.

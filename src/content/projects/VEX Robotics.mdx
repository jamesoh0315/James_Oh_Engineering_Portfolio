## 2. Autonomous Soccer Bot

### Overall design

Designed with a frontal intake to grab tennis balls and load them onto the catapult. It
carries **2 parallel shaft encoders**, **1 perpendicular shaft encoder**, and an **IMU** for
path tracking.

<Figures
	height={460}
	items={[{ src: soccerBot, alt: 'Overhead view of the soccer bot showing its omni wheels and rubber-band catapult' }]}
/>

### Testing

With a pre-sampled path generated using **Bézier curves**, the bot tracks its own position
while constantly correcting its deviation, in order to shoot at the desired location.

<Figures
	height={240}
	caption="Tracking the sampled path and shooting from the target position."
	items={[{ video: '/media/soccer-bot-shoot.mp4', poster: '/media/soccer-bot-shoot-poster.jpg', alt: 'The soccer bot driving a curved path and launching a tennis ball at the goal' }]}
/>

### Programming

Program modified and developed under instruction, using **C++** in the VEX V5 programming
environment.

- `updatePosition()` — position tracking odometry, taking the input of 2 parallel encoders
  and the robot heading angle returned by the IMU.
- `controlMotors(double now)` — controller following points on the Bézier curve path,
  calculating the current deviation from the next point and adjusting individual wheel
  speeds accordingly.

## 3. Velocity Controlled Curling Bot

### Objective

Using the principle of momentum, the objective was to deliver the curling stone travelling
accurately at the desired velocity, in order to re-create one of the greatest curling shots
in history.

<Figures
	height={460}
	caption="The shot geometry: stone positions and the delivery path to re-create."
	items={[{ src: curlingDiagram, alt: 'Plotted diagram of the curling shot showing stone positions and the delivery path' }]}
/>

### Programming

Program modified and developed under instruction, using **C++** in the VEX V5 programming
environment.

`setVel(double setPoint, double direction)` is a **PID controller** taking two parameters:

- **setPoint** — the desired velocity, compared against the calculated current velocity,
  accounting for wheel circumference and gear ratio.
- **direction** — the desired heading. Mostly used to correct the robot's path, as it often
  deviates due to physical misalignment.
